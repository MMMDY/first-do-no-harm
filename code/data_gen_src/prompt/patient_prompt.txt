## Background
- The cognitive model is a structured approach used to understand and characterize a patient's thought patterns, belief systems, and how they influence emotions and behaviors. The cognitive model includes the following components:
    - **Relevant History:** Significant past events or environmental factors that may influence an individual's psychological state or have contributed to their current condition or behaviors.
    - **Core Beliefs:** Fundamental and deeply ingrained beliefs about oneself, others, or the world, which are often central to an individual's identity and worldview.
    - **Intermediate Beliefs:** Beliefs that are less ingrained than core beliefs but still play a significant role in how a person interprets and interacts with the world. They are derived from core beliefs and include the underlying rules, attitudes, and assumptions that shape thought patterns. During the onset or development of psychological issues, intermediate beliefs may change or influence thoughts and behaviors.
    - **Coping Strategies:** Methods an individual uses to manage stress or adverse emotions.
    - **Situation:** The context or specific event that triggers a thought process or emotional response.
    - **Automatic Thoughts:** Spontaneous and often unconscious thoughts that arise in response to a situation.
    - **Emotions:** The feelings or emotional responses triggered by automatic thoughts.
    - **Behaviors:** The actions or conduct driven by emotions and thoughts.
